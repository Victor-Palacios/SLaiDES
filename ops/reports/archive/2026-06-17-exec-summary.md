# Executive summary — 2026-06-17

## ELI5

Imagine a box of LEGO slide-shapes that helpers can snap together to make a
presentation. Tonight we added six new shapes to the box: a row of numbered steps,
a timeline of plans laid out in lanes, a funnel that gets narrower toward the bottom,
a pyramid that gets wider toward the bottom, a four-box grid for sorting ideas, and a
classic "strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, threats" four-square. Every shape is
built from plain rectangles and words, so a computer can measure it and promise
nothing spills off the edge — no guessing, no peeking at a picture. We tested all six,
and they all fit perfectly. The big box now holds 34 of the planned 40 shapes.

We also did a bit of "homework": we keep a reading list of trustworthy research about
what makes a slide look good. We chased down every entry we hadn't double-checked yet
and confirmed it's real, and we found two more solid studies to add. The list now has
15 fully-checked sources.

## Broad strokes

This was a steady, on-plan building session. The project's goal is a library of 40
reusable slide designs that a computer can assemble and verify without ever rendering
a picture — correctness is proven by measurement, not by looking. Tonight completed
the "process & shape" family of designs, the trickier group because funnels, pyramids
and quadrant charts are usually drawn freehand. We kept the rule that everything is
made of measurable colored rectangles and text, so the automatic checker can still
guarantee a clean result. All six new designs passed every test and produced
clean, viewable example documents.

That brings the library to 34 of 40 finished, with one group of six left (people,
images, and comparison grids) before the collection is complete.

Alongside the building, we maintain a small research bibliography that justifies, with
published evidence, how the tool decides whether a slide is well-designed. We cleared
the last of the "not-yet-verified" entries — every source on the list is now confirmed
against its original publication — and added two more respected studies, one on
automatic page-layout scoring and one on measuring interface beauty. The list stands
at 15 verified sources, on track toward the target of about 20. Keeping this honest
matters: it means the tool's design opinions are grounded in real research, not made
up, and that's the foundation for later tuning.
