### Standing research — info_density direction grounded with a controlled experiment (commit `48a19db`, board T-053)

- **Searched the cognitive-load slide literature for a numeric word threshold.** Found the
  consistent guidance is a **minimization principle** ("as few words as possible"; fewer words →
  better comprehension), **not** a single empirical words-per-slide maximum. So slidekit's
  45-word full-credit / 130-word zero-credit band and the 0.45/0.85 coverage band **stay
  HEURISTIC** — the same honest outcome as the #19 color_harmony 30° and #22 hierarchy 1.5×
  groundings (direction sourced; exact number is our design choice).
- **Added a genuinely-novel, high-quality primary source.** **REFERENCES.md #23 — Wecker,
  "Slide presentations as speech suppressors: When and why learners miss oral information"
  (_Computers & Education_ 59(2):260–273, 2012; DOI 10.1016/j.compedu.2012.01.013)**, verified
  from the ERIC record EJ966972 + Crossref/ACM DL DOI metadata. It is a **controlled experiment**
  (n=209; three conditions — no-slides / regular PowerPoint / concise) finding a **"speech
  suppression effect"**: text-dense regular slides reduce retention of *orally-presented*
  information — and, notably, this is **not** explained by cognitive overload but by
  **dysfunctional allocation of attention**, an effect **avoided by concise slides**. This backs
  info_density's direction more strongly than #20 (an experiment, not design criteria) and adds a
  distinct *mechanism*. Per the post-target standing-task rule (add only genuinely novel works
  that close a flagged gap), this closes the info_density direction-grounding gap.
- **Decision: info_density direction is now doubly-sourced (#20 + #23); bands stay HEURISTIC.**
  **No metric behaviour change** — did NOT move the bands; that is recalibration and belongs with
  the DEFERRED weight calibration (needs a labelled dataset). Updated REFERENCES.md (#23 + header
